About me

Hi, I'm Vlad! Welcome to my humble webpage, don't be afraid of my hedgehog, but do not scare him either.

Since October 2026, I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at the Astronomical Institute of the Czech Academy of Sciences (ASU CAS), working with Dr. Michal Dovčiak. My current research focuses on the spectro-polarimetric and timing properties of X-ray binaries (XRBs) and their connection to relativistic plasma physics. I am also an active member of Working Group 4 in the EXPAND COST Action.

Previously, I spent a year as an independent guest postdoctoral researcher at Newcastle University and Durham University, collaborating with Dr. Adam Ingram on the X-MAPS project. My work there focused deeply on the timing properties of black hole XRBs, funded by a research abroad grant from the Finnish Academy of Science and Letters.

I earned my PhD in the High Energy Astrophysics group at the University of Turku (UTU) under the supervision of doc. Alexandra Veledina and prof. Juri Poutanen, focusing on X-ray polarimetry and observations from NASA's IXPE satellite. In parallel with my doctoral studies, I focused on relativistic plasma physics and turbulence: in 2023, I was a predoctoral fellow in the Center for Computational Astrophysics (CCA) at the Flatiron Institute, collaborating with prof. Joonas Nättilä and Dr. Vladimir Zhdankin. Building on this, I continued working with Joonas during my non-military service (2024–2025) in his Computational Plasma Astrophysics group at the University of Helsinki (UH), contributing to the ILLUMINATOR project and developing the simulation code runko. I received my Master's degree in astronomy from Saint Petersburg State University.

Research Focus: Broadly, I study black hole and neutron star X-ray binaries. My work bridges observational signatures and theoretical models by combining radiative transfer and Comptonization in hot atmospheres, numerical simulations of magnetized plasma turbulence, and analytical/numerical ray-tracing of polarized X-rays.
