What is this diagram?

An attractor of f(x+T) = F(f(x), x) lives over the circle θ = x mod 2π, so it cannot be drawn as a single number per parameter. The slice cuts it with the vertical line θ = θ* (the shaded band in “Graph f(θ)”, the highlighted points in “Attractor in ℂ”) and plots what lies on that line, for every parameter along the segment — the analogue of the logistic bifurcation diagram.

Each dot is an orbit value zn whose angle θn = nT mod 2π falls within ±w of θ* — the spread. The line under the sliders says how many of the recorded orbit points fall in the window (the angles are the same for every parameter, about N·w/π of the last N), and how many of them, newest first, are drawn. An invariant graph with k sheets meets the line in exactly k points, so the diagram shows k curves: the number of lines is the number of sheets. Doublings appear as a curve splitting in two; a vertical smear means the section is no longer a finite set — a strange nonchaotic attractor (λ < 0) or chaos (λ > 0). Moving θ* changes where the curves sit, never how many there are.

A narrow spread gives sharp curves from few points; widening it thickens each curve into a band of height ≈ |f′|·2w (at w = π every orbit point is drawn, and the column is the whole range of f). The angles inside a window are not evenly spread: when T/2π is close to p/q, the θn stack into q columns 2π/q apart, so a window narrower than π/q may hold a single column visited many times.

A slice can be a straight line, a polyline (its corners are dotted vertical lines in the diagram) or a smoothed freehand trace; the horizontal axis is then the position along it, by arc length. Click the diagram, or drag the “Move along” slider under the plane, to analyse the parameter at that position.

Every candidate sheet structure (c components, each a d-fold cover, shift s) is scored by fitting the orbit with a smooth function on that cover; the score is the unexplained variance fraction. The smallest k = c·d below the threshold wins and seeds Newton.

This k only limits the search here and the verdict above; the plane colouring uses Max sheets k in the left panel.

For F = g(x)·f or F = f + L(x) the orbit has a closed form. Write log g = λ + iβ + L̃(θ) + iwθ and solve ψ(θ+T) − ψ(θ) = L̃(θ) in Fourier: ψₘ = L̃ₘ / (e^{imT} − 1). Then z_n = z₀·e^{(λ+iβ)n}·e^{ψ(θ_n) − ψ(0)}.
